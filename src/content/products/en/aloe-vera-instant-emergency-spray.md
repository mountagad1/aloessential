The Emergency Spray has a misleading name, or perhaps too apt a one: it makes you think of a first aid kit. In fact it is a cosmetic body spray that is very easy to use. You spray it on, let it dry, and your skin feels comfortable again. That is its main strength: it lets you care for skin you would rather not touch.

## What it contains

The base is almost entirely aloe: 83% aloe vera gel according to LR, plus a fermented aloe gel. Then come glycerin for moisture, allantoin, often used in soothing products, and eleven plant extracts, from chamomile and calendula to sage and thyme. Propolis completes the formula.

The spray leaves a light film that soothes and cools. LR's product sheet also talks about "regenerated" skin: we stick to cosmetic functions, which are moisturising, soothing and protecting.

## How to fit it into your routine

- **After the sun**, on skin that feels hot, is its most obvious use. Kept in the fridge, it is even more pleasant. Our article [Sensitive skin after the sun: a soothing routine](/en/articles/after-sun-care-for-sensitive-skin/) covers the other steps.
- **For very dry hands**, LR suggests a ten-minute soak, twice a week.
- **For a tight-feeling scalp**, apply it to clean skin and massage it in, without rinsing.
- **For a cooling moment**, soak a cold cloth with it and place it on your legs or closed eyes.

## Who it is not right for

- **To treat a burn, a wound or severe sunburn**: it is not a first aid product. If there are blisters, weeping, a high temperature or severe pain, get medical advice.
- **If you are allergic to bee products** (propolis) or to one of the plants in the formula, especially the daisy family (chamomile, calendula, yarrow, dandelion).
- **If you are looking for a very rich moisturiser**: the spray moisturises and cools, but very dry skin will also need a richer cream.
