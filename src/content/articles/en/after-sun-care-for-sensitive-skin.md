A day outdoors, at the beach or in the garden, and by the evening your skin feels tight, warm and a little red. People with sensitive skin know this well: the sun puts it to the test, even with protection. A few simple steps help you feel comfortable again. They do not replace good sun protection, and they do not treat real sunburn.

## First, know the difference

Skin that feels a bit warm and tight calls for a care routine. **Sunburn** is a burn to the skin.

Ask for an urgent GP appointment or get help from NHS 111 if, after being in the sun:

- your skin is blistered or swollen;
- your temperature is very high, or you feel hot, cold or shivery;
- you feel very tired, dizzy or sick, or you have a headache or muscle cramps;
- a baby or young child has sunburn.

A pharmacist can also advise you on sunburn treatment. In these situations, cosmetics are not the answer.

## 1. Cool down without stressing the skin

A **lukewarm** shower, rather than cold or hot, eases the feeling of heat without shocking the skin. For a few days, avoid scrubs, exfoliating mitts and harsh foaming cleansers.

Pat yourself dry with a clean towel; do not rub.

## 2. Cleanse gently

By the evening, your skin has collected sunscreen, sweat, salt or chlorine. A gentle, fragrance-free cleanser is all you need. On the face, micellar water or a cleansing milk for sensitive skin, followed by a rinse with water, works very well.

## 3. Moisturise generously

Sun, wind and sea or pool water dry the skin out. Now is the time to give it water and something to hold it in:

- an **aloe vera gel** feels instantly cool and moisturises without greasiness;
- a **spray** is handy for large areas, your back or shoulders, and on skin you would rather not touch too much;
- a **richer cream**, applied afterwards, helps limit moisture loss and suits dry skin.

Keep your gel in the fridge: the cooling effect is stronger. To choose your gel, see our checklist in [How to choose an aloe vera gel](/en/articles/how-to-choose-aloe-vera-gel/).

## 4. Avoid anything that irritates

On sun-warmed skin, it is best to leave out for a few days:

- fragrance and alcohol in skincare;
- exfoliating ingredients (AHA or BHA acids) and retinol;
- shaving and waxing on red areas.

## 5. Drink, and protect yourself the next day

Drink regularly, mainly water: sun and heat increase fluid loss. Water-rich foods such as watermelon, cucumber or tomatoes help too.

The next day your skin is still more fragile. Cover up (clothes, hat), stay in the shade during the hottest hours, and reapply a suitable sunscreen every two hours and after swimming.

## The routine at a glance

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Back indoors | Lukewarm shower, pat dry |
| In the evening | Gentle cleanser, then moisturising gel or spray |
| Before bed | Richer cream on dry areas |
| The next day | Sunscreen, shade, covering clothes |

## In short

After the sun, your skin needs gentleness and water, not a complicated routine. The best after-sun product is still the one you never need: sensible exposure and well-applied protection.
