Getting up without stiffness, climbing the stairs without thinking, spending a whole afternoon in the garden: mobility is a comfort you mostly notice when it fades. The good news is that much of what keeps it going comes down to everyday habits.

## Movement, your first ally

Joints are made to move. Cartilage has no blood supply: it partly depends on movement to get what it needs. A joint that stays still tends to stiffen.

The World Health Organization recommends that adults do:

- **150 to 300 minutes** a week of moderate-intensity activity (brisk walking, cycling, swimming), or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity;
- **muscle-strengthening** exercises on at least two days a week;
- for people over 65, varied activities combining **balance** and strength on at least three days a week, to reduce the risk of falls.

You do not have to do it all at once: ten minutes of walking several times a day counts too.

## Muscles that take the load off your joints

Strong muscles stabilise your joints and absorb part of the impact. Your thighs protect your knees; your stomach and back muscles support your spine.

A few simple exercises to do at home:

1. **Stand up from a chair** without using your hands, 10 times, slowly.
2. **Rise onto your toes** while holding the back of a chair, 15 times.
3. **The bridge**: lying on your back, knees bent, lift your hips, hold for 5 seconds, 10 times.

Start gently, and stop if an exercise causes sharp pain.

## Small habits that change everything

- **Warm up** for a few minutes before sport or gardening.
- **Change position**: get up every 30 to 60 minutes when you work sitting down.
- **Lift sensibly**: keep loads close to your body and bend your knees rather than your back.
- **Wear good shoes** for walking, with decent cushioning.
- **Watch your weight**: every extra kilo adds load on your hips and knees.

## Your plate counts too

A varied diet provides nutrients that are useful for bones and muscles. Among the authorised health claims:

- **vitamin C** contributes to normal collagen formation for the normal function of cartilage and bones;
- **vitamin D** contributes to the maintenance of normal bones and normal muscle function;
- **calcium** is needed for the maintenance of normal bones.

You will find vitamin C in fresh fruit and vegetables, calcium in dairy foods, some mineral waters and green vegetables, and vitamin D mainly from sunlight and oily fish.

## What about "joint" supplements?

Many supplements contain glucosamine, chondroitin, collagen or plant extracts. It is worth knowing that, to date, **none of these substances has an authorised health claim** for joints: EFSA found the evidence insufficient for glucosamine and chondroitin.

When a supplement can legally highlight a benefit, it is thanks to a vitamin or mineral it contains in a significant amount, such as vitamin C. Read the ingredients and check which nutrients actually carry the claim.

Take care if you are allergic to shellfish, as glucosamine is often made from it, or if you take anticoagulants: speak to your GP or pharmacist.

## When to see your GP

Short-lived stiffness after unusual effort is common. See your GP, though, if you notice:

- a swollen, warm or red joint;
- pain lasting more than a few weeks or waking you at night;
- morning stiffness lasting more than half an hour;
- pain after a fall or injury.

## In short

To stay mobile, the winning trio stays the same: move every day, keep your muscles strong and eat a varied diet. Supplements can add to your diet, but never replace movement or medical advice when pain sets in.
