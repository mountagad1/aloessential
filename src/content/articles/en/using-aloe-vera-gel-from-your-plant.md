An aloe vera on the windowsill makes you want to use its gel. You can, on your skin, as long as you take a few precautions. The main one: separate the gel from the latex properly.

## Gel and latex: what to know before you cut

The aloe leaf contains two substances. The **gel**, in the centre, is clear, cool and mostly water. The **latex**, yellow and bitter, oozes just under the skin of the leaf when you cut it. That is what contains aloin.

Taken by mouth, the latex is a strong laxative and can cause cramps and diarrhoea. The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) has also classified non-decolourised whole-leaf extract of aloe vera as "possibly carcinogenic" to humans. That is why commercial drinks use filtered gel with these compounds removed.

**The practical conclusion: homemade gel is for your skin only.** Do not drink it or add it to smoothies.

## Extracting the gel, step by step

1. **Choose an outer leaf**, nice and thick, on a mature plant. Cut it at the base with a clean knife.
2. **Let the latex drain**: stand the leaf upright, cut end down, in a glass for about ten minutes. The yellow liquid runs out.
3. **Rinse** the leaf under clean water.
4. **Trim off the spiny edges**, then carefully peel away the green skin on the top and underside.
5. **Rinse the gel** to remove any last traces of yellow.

## Applying it to your skin

- **Patch test first**: a small amount on the inside of your elbow, then wait 24 hours. Aloe allergies do happen.
- Apply a thin layer to clean skin, for example after the sun or on a dry patch. The gel leaves a cool film, then dries without greasiness.
- Stop if you notice redness, itching or stinging.

Aloe gel moisturises and soothes: these are cosmetic effects. It does not treat a burn, a wound or a skin condition. A widespread burn, or skin that weeps or blisters, needs medical advice.

## Storing it

Without preservatives, homemade gel goes off quickly. Keep it in the fridge in a small clean, closed jar and use it **within a few days**. Throw it away if its colour, smell or appearance changes. The unused part of the leaf also keeps in the fridge, wrapped, for next time.

If you want to use gel every day, a shop-bought, stabilised and tested gel is more practical. Our checklist for choosing one is in [How to choose an aloe vera gel](/en/articles/how-to-choose-aloe-vera-gel/).

## Precautions to remember

- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding**: do not take aloe by mouth; for use on the skin, ask for advice if in doubt.
- **Children**: keep leaves and latex out of their reach.
- **Pets**: aloe vera is toxic to cats and dogs.
- **Treated plants**: do not use gel from a plant that has been sprayed with pesticides.

## In short

A leaf well drained of its latex, rinsed gel, a patch test and quick use: that is the right way to enjoy your plant. To keep it thriving, see [How to grow aloe vera in a pot](/en/articles/how-to-grow-aloe-vera-in-a-pot/).
