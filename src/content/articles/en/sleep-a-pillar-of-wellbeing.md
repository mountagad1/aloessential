Sleep affects your mood, concentration, appetite and recovery. The good news: a few habits have a real effect on its quality.

## Regularity first

Going to bed and getting up at similar times, weekends included, is one of the most reliable tips. Your body clock loves consistency.

## A bedroom that helps

- **Cool**: around 18 °C.
- **Dark**: blackout curtains or an eye mask.
- **Quiet**: earplugs can help in a town or city.

## Light and screens

Bright light in the evening delays falling asleep. Dim the lights an hour before bed and keep screens away from your bed. A paper book or a few minutes of slow breathing make good wind-down rituals.

## Watch out for stimulants

Caffeine can act for several hours after your last coffee. If you sleep badly, try not to have any after early afternoon. Alcohol, for its part, sometimes helps you fall asleep but breaks up your sleep.

## When to see your GP

Trouble sleeping several nights a week for more than a month, heavy snoring or sleepiness during the day are worth discussing with your GP.
