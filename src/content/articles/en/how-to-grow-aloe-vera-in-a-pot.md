Aloe vera is one of the easiest houseplants to keep… as long as you do not love it too much. This succulent comes from hot, dry regions: what kills it is almost never neglect, but too much water.

## Plenty of light

Aloe vera needs sun. Place it by the brightest window in the house, ideally south- or west-facing. It copes well near a radiator and likes dry air.

Without enough light it becomes leggy: its leaves grow thinner, paler and lean towards the window. If you put it outside on a patio or balcony for the summer, do it gradually: moved suddenly from indoors into full sun, it can scorch. Turn the pot a quarter turn now and then so it grows straight.

## Which compost and which pot?

Its roots hate sitting in water: choosing the compost and the pot is all about letting water drain away and the compost dry quickly.

**The compost.** Two options:

- ready-made **cactus and succulent compost**;
- a **home mix**: half multi-purpose compost, preferably peat-free, half coarse sand or horticultural grit. That is the proportion the Royal Horticultural Society recommends.

Avoid plain multi-purpose compost on its own, which stays wet for too long.

**The pot.**

- It must have **drainage holes**, and the saucer should be emptied after each watering.
- **Terracotta** lets the compost dry faster than plastic or glazed ceramic.
- Choose one **only slightly bigger than the root ball**. A pot that is too big holds moisture and encourages root rot: when repotting, go up no more than two sizes at a time.

## Water little, and only when it is dry

The rule is simple: water only when the compost is dry all the way down, then water thoroughly and empty the saucer. In practice, that often means watering every two or three weeks in spring and summer, depending on the warmth and the size of the pot. In winter, water very little.

Soft, brown or translucent leaves at the base are the classic sign of overwatering: let it dry out completely and water less often.

For a rhythm suited to your pot, your room and the season, try [our aloe vera watering planner](/en/aloe-vera-watering-planner/).

## Cold is its enemy

Aloe vera cannot survive frost. If it spends the summer outside, bring it back in as soon as the nights turn cool, usually by early autumn. In the UK it lives indoors for the winter: a bright, warm room suits it all year round.

A little liquid succulent feed once a month from May to August is enough. No feed in winter.

## Repotting your aloe vera

Repot in spring, when roots grow out of the drainage hole or the plant becomes top-heavy and tips over.

1. Wait until the compost is dry, then ease the plant out of its pot.
2. Shake off the old crumbly compost and cut away any soft or black roots.
3. Set the plant in a slightly bigger pot with free-draining compost, at the same depth as before.
4. Wait a few days before watering, so any small root wounds can dry.

## Making more plants: offsets

A mature plant produces small offsets, or "pups", at its base. To pot them up:

1. In spring or early summer, take the parent plant out of its pot.
2. Separate the offsets while they are still small, with a few roots if possible.
3. Pot them up on their own in cactus compost, and wait a few days before a first light watering.

It is also a good time to give the parent plant a slightly bigger pot if its roots are crowded.

## What the leaves are telling you

| What you see | Most likely cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft, translucent leaves, or leaves turning brown at the base | Too much water | Let it dry out completely, water less often, check the pot drains |
| Thin, wrinkled leaves that plump up again after watering | Too little water | Water thoroughly when the compost is dry |
| Thin, pale leaves leaning towards the window | Not enough light | Move it closer to a sunny window |
| Brown or scorched patches after a spell outdoors | Sunburn | Get the plant used to full sun gradually |
| Small white cottony clumps or tiny brown shells | Mealybugs or scale insects | Isolate the plant and remove the insects by hand, for example with a cotton bud |

## Care through the seasons

| Season | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Spring | Repot if needed, separate offsets, start watering again as growth resumes |
| Summer | Water when the compost is dry; a little succulent feed once a month (May to August); move outdoors gradually if you like |
| Autumn | Water less often; bring the plant in before the nights turn cold |
| Winter | Bright, mild room; hardly any water; no feed |

## Keep it away from pets

Aloe vera is **toxic to cats and dogs**. The yellow latex in its leaves can cause vomiting, diarrhoea and lethargy. Keep it out of your pets' reach, and call your vet if one of them has eaten some.

## And if you want to use its gel?

A well-established plant can spare a leaf now and then for use on the skin. It is worth knowing how to go about it: we explain in [Using the gel from your own aloe vera plant](/en/articles/using-aloe-vera-gel-from-your-plant/).

## In short

Sun, compost that dries quickly, little water and no frost: follow these four rules and an aloe vera will live for years and multiply on its own.
