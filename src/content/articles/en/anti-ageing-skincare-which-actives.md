The anti-ageing aisle is full of active ingredients, percentages and promises. To find your way, start from a simple idea: a cream works on how your skin **looks** (hydration, radiance, suppleness, the appearance of fine lines). It does not turn back time. Well chosen and well used, though, it can make a real difference day to day.

## Active no. 1: sun protection

UV rays cause much of the visible ageing of the skin: wrinkles, dark spots, loss of firmness. A broad-spectrum sunscreen, applied to your face every day, even when it is cloudy, is the most effective anti-ageing habit there is.

If you were to change only one thing in your routine, this would be it.

## The main families of actives

### Retinoids

**Retinol** and its derivatives are the most studied anti-ageing actives in cosmetics. They improve the appearance of wrinkles and evenness of skin tone.

- Use them **in the evening**, as they make the skin more sensitive to the sun.
- Introduce them gradually (two evenings a week to start with), as they can irritate.
- **Avoid them during pregnancy** and breastfeeding.

The European Union has also set maximum concentrations for retinol in cosmetics.

### Vitamin C

An antioxidant, it helps protect the skin from external stresses and adds radiance. It is best used **in the morning**, under your sunscreen. Its pure form is fragile: choose opaque bottles and close them tightly.

### Niacinamide

Also called vitamin B3, it suits most skin types well. It helps even out skin tone, visibly tighten pores and strengthen the skin barrier. It is a good place to start for sensitive skin.

### Hyaluronic acid

Found naturally in the skin, it holds on to water. In cosmetics, it hydrates and visibly plumps fine lines caused by dehydration. It suits all skin types.

### Exfoliating acids (AHAs)

Glycolic or lactic acid refine skin texture and revive radiance. They make the skin more sensitive to the sun: sunscreen is a must.

### Peptides and oils

Peptides and plant oils nourish and soften the skin. They are often combined with other actives in all-in-one creams.

## Which active for which need?

| Your concern | Actives to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Fine lines from dehydration | Hyaluronic acid, glycerin, aloe vera |
| Established wrinkles | Retinoids, sun protection |
| Dull complexion | Vitamin C, AHAs |
| Dark spots | Sun protection, vitamin C, niacinamide |
| Dry or tight skin | Plant oils, butters, ceramides |
| Sensitive skin | Niacinamide, aloe vera, short fragrance-free formulas |

## Building your routine

1. **Morning**: gentle cleanser, vitamin C serum if you like, moisturiser, sunscreen.
2. **Evening**: remove make-up, then an active treatment (retinol or AHAs, not both on the same evening), a richer cream.
3. **One new active at a time**, with a few weeks' trial before judging the result.
4. **A patch test** in the crook of your elbow or behind your ear before the first use on your face.

## Take promises with a pinch of salt

In the UK, as in the EU, cosmetic claims must be backed up and must not mislead. A product can "improve the appearance of wrinkles" or "hydrate", not "erase" them or "repair" the skin in a medical sense. Figures such as "-30% wrinkles" come from specific tests on a small number of people: they show a trend, not a guarantee.

## In short

Good anti-ageing care starts with daily sun protection, then one or two actives chosen for your needs and introduced gradually. Consistency matters more than the number of products.
