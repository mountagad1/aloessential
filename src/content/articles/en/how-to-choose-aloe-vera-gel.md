Faced with dozens of "aloe vera" gels at the chemist, the supermarket or the health food shop, it is hard to know where to start. A few simple checks help you choose with confidence.

## Look at the percentage… and what it refers to

A gel labelled "99% aloe vera" sounds ideal. But what does it actually mean: juice reconstituted from powder, fresh stabilised gel, diluted concentrate? Transparent brands say which form they use and where it comes from.

## Read the ingredient list

The INCI list is ordered by decreasing concentration. Aloe vera (*Aloe barbadensis leaf juice*) should come first. Be wary of formulas where it appears after water, alcohol or fragrance.

## Avoid unnecessary additives

A preservative is essential in any water-based product. Green colourings, synthetic fragrance and denatured alcohol, on the other hand, do nothing for your skin and may irritate it.

## Certifications: a useful pointer

Organic certifications and the International Aloe Science Council seal guarantee a degree of control over aloe content and growing practices. They do not tell you everything, but they are a good starting point.

## Store it well

- Close the bottle properly after use.
- Keep it away from heat; in summer, the fridge adds a welcome cooling effect.
- Respect the period after opening, shown by the small open-jar symbol on the pack (for example "12M").

A good gel is not necessarily the most expensive: it is the one with a short, clear ingredient list that matches how you plan to use it.
